## Where the decisions live

**D1–D29 remain in [`13_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`](../13_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) Part A**, which is still the single index of what has been decided. These ADRs carry the fuller reasoning for the five that were consequential enough to need it — a gate outcome, a reversal, a reuse challenge answered after the fact, a reversal of the reversal, and a simplification of the authoring surface.

When they disagree, the ADR is the record of *why* and Part A is the record of *what*. Neither should be edited without the other.

## Writing the next one

Follow the existing shape: context, decision, evidence, consequences, and an explicit **revisit-if**. Two habits are worth keeping, because both are already load-bearing in 0002:

- **Preserve superseded reasoning rather than deleting it.** D9's original argument was correct given what was known; the premise moved, not the logic. A reader who cannot see that will not trust the next reversal either.
- **State what the decision does *not* license.** D27 permits measuring free VRAM and continues to forbid predicting a model's consumption. Decisions drift at their edges, and the edge is the part worth writing down.
- **Declare the evidence class, and name the falsifier.** 0001 rested on executed spikes; 0003 rests on documented behaviour and one structural argument, and says so on its face. A reader must be able to tell how much weight a decision can bear — and a decision with no stated falsifier is an opinion.
- **Give a reuse candidate the full treatment or none.** 0003 exists because a one-paragraph dismissal invited a challenge that took a document to answer. Score against R1–R4 and against the decision log, or do not record a verdict at all.
- **Read the candidate's manual before saying what it cannot do.** 0003's first version asserted a limitation that one documentation page disproved. The falsifier caught it within hours — which is the system working — but the citation should have been checked first. **Quote the source; do not characterise it from memory.**
- **Watch the direction of successive corrections.** 0003 was narrowed three times, always the same way. When every new fact moves a decision toward the alternative, the decision is probably wrong even if no single fact overturns it — **say so in the ADR rather than letting the reader assemble the pattern themselves.** *(Postscript: 0003 survived anyway, but only after being re-argued on a different axis. If a decision needs three rescues, check whether it is being defended on the wrong grounds.)*
- **Separate the premise from the reasoning, and mark which is which.** **D9 has now been reversed twice, and the logic was sound every time** — what moved was a fact about the deployment, inferred rather than confirmed. 0002 built a v1 feature on an inference about the shared DGX; 0004 removed it when the requester stated the actual demand. **If a decision rests on a fact about the deployment, quote whoever knows it, or label it as inferred.**
- **Ask what a decision costs to keep, not only whether it is right.** 0004 exists because [`16_COMPLEXITY_AUDIT.md`](../16_COMPLEXITY_AUDIT.md) asked that question of every decision at once. Individually justified increments still sum to a system nobody wants to maintain.
- **When a decision splits a surface in two, check whether collapsing it is cheaper than drawing the line accurately.** 0005 was proposed as *"narrow D15 to the cases where the restriction is genuine"* — which would have left two parameter classes and a rule for choosing between them. The requester's *"if it's simpler to have everything batched then let's make it batched"* removed the distinction instead. **Refining a boundary is the reflex; deleting it is usually the better move, and nobody proposes it unless asked.**
